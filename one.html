Kushti, also known as Pehlwani, is an ancient Indian form of traditional mud wrestling. Competitors, called pehlwans, face off in a clay pit prepared with turmeric, ghee, and buttermilk. The objective is to pin the opponent's shoulders to the ground. Matches are tests of strength, skill, and discipline.


The Arena and Rules

The Pit:

The wrestling arena, typically circular or square, is known as the akhara. The soil is treated with ayurvedic herbs, oils, and red ochre to give it a smooth texture that absorbs impact.


Uniform

Wrestlers wear only a traditional cotton loincloth called a langot, which prevents hernias and allows for grips during the match.


Match Play

There are no rounds or points in a traditional Kushti bout. The match continues until one wrestler pins their opponent's shoulders and hips flat to the ground simultaneously. Submission, knockout, or referee stoppage are also methods of victory. Strikes and kicks are strictly forbidden, focusing the sport entirely on grappling, throws, and locks.


You can explore more about this traditional martial art through the Pehlwani Wikipedia page or read about its cultural significance on the Traditional Games Federation of India.
Lifestyle and Training
The Akhara:

Akharas serve as both gyms and communal living spaces where wrestlers train and follow a strict, disciplined lifestyle.


Diet:

Pehlwans follow a highly nutritious diet rich in milk, ghee, almonds, and lentils, while strictly avoiding alcohol, tobacco, and excessively spiced foods.


Training Methods:

Daily routines involve intense physical exercises, including bodyweight exercises like dands (Hindu push-ups) and baithaks (deep knee bends),as well as heavy lifting with traditional stones (nal).


History and Origins

Developed primarily during the Mughal Empire, Kushti combines ancient Indian malla-yuddha with Persian koshti pahlevani. It is more than just a sport; it is considered a way of life that demands spiritual focus, rigorous dieting, and strict social unity. Practitioners, known as pehlwans, train under the guidance of a master teacher, respectfully referred to as an ustad or guru.

🏛️ The Arena and Equipment

Akhara / Taleem:

The traditional mud pit where wrestlers train and compete. The clay floor is mixed with salt, lemon, ghee (clarified butter), and buttermilk to keep it soft and prevent injuries.


Langot:

A simple cotton loincloth worn by wrestlers (Pehlwans) to allow maximum movement and prevent physical strain like hernias.
